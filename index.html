      What you'll actually learn

      Control the center

      Pieces in the middle of the board have more reach. Almost every opening begins by fighting for e4, d4, e5, and d5.

      Develop before you attack

      Get your knights and bishops out before launching anything. An undeveloped army loses — in chess and everywhere else.

      Keep your king safe

      Castle early, usually within the first eight moves. A king in the center of the board is a king in danger.

      Don't move the same piece twice

      Unless you have a very good reason, develop each piece once and move on. Time is a resource in the opening.

      Why the first fifteen moves?

      Grandmasters know the opening cold. Beginners often don't — and the gap decides most games before move 20. The good news: the first fifteen moves reward understanding far more than memorization. Learn the ideas, and the moves follow.

      This site pairs a real chess engine (Stockfish, running in your browser) with hand-written notes on classical opening theory. The engine checks the math. The coach explains the meaning.
